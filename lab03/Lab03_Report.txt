1. The Race Condition (Phase 2):
In the `race_condition.py` script, what exactly causes the final weight to be less than the expected value? Explain the role of the OS Scheduler in this error.

Answer: The weight is less than expected because temp = shared_weight and shared_weight = temp + 1 is a non-atomic operation (read-modify-write). The OS Scheduler switches threads mid-execution (during time.sleep), causing one thread to overwrite another thread's updates, resulting in lost data.

2. Mutex vs. Semaphore (Phase 3):
In `producer_consumer.py`:
- What is the specific job of the `mutex`?

Answer: Mutex: Ensures Mutual Exclusion by allowing only one thread to access the critical section (buffer array and indices) at a time.

- What is the specific job of the `empty` and `full` semaphores? 
Answer: Semaphores (empty/full): Handle signaling and counting available buffer slots (empty) and items (full).

(Why can't we just use a Mutex for everything?)

Answer: Why not just Mutex: A Mutex only locks data; it cannot block a thread when the buffer is empty/full or notify another thread when space/data becomes available.

3. Deadlock Awareness:
In the Producer function, we wrote:
    empty.acquire()
    mutex.acquire()
What would happen if we swapped these two lines? (mutex first, then empty). Imagine the buffer is completely full. Explain the potential OS problem.

Answer: A Deadlock will occur. If the buffer is full, the Producer locks the mutex first, then gets blocked waiting for empty.acquire(). However, the Consumer can never run full.acquire() and free space because it is blocked waiting for the Producer to release the mutex, causing both threads to freeze forever.
